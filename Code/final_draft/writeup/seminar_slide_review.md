## Content points to preserve or clarify while shortening

1. **Keep the original novelty measure.** Explain that it is novelty relative to last week's basket: `N = 1 - previous own-flavor share`. It reduces to a switching indicator for single-flavor baskets; it is gradual for mixed baskets. Do not describe every estimated novelty effect as a literal binary switch or promise a one-time bonus.
2. **Explain the unit of WTP.** The approximately $1.75 magnitude is for a full unit increase in N, not every observed flavor purchase or every basket change. Stock WTP is per unit of cumulative weekly own-flavor share, not automatically per package.
3. **Do not bury all definitions.** Main slides must retain an intuitive distinction between recent exposure (N) and accumulated own-flavor shares (H), and a sentence on how the outside option is constructed. The formal cases and implementation details can move.
4. **Give identifying variation main-body time.** Distinguish stable flavor tastes from within-household changes in recent and accumulated exposure, explain the role of price controls, and avoid implying that including history by itself establishes causal state dependence.
5. **Use the uncontrolled fit throughout the main analysis.** Demand is already presented without controls. The pricing job and full-rebuild pricing step were configured to use the controlled fit; those results need replacement from an uncontrolled-only pricing rerun. Keep controlled demand estimates as appendix robustness results. Do not retain the old pricing headline numbers without checking the new run.
6. **Separate the two revenue questions.** The 13.35% plain and 7.26% berry gains compare personalized policy with no coupons. They are not estimates of the incremental value of learning novelty type. The fixed-belief comparisons address the latter. The common-offer benchmark is also essential to assessing personalization.
7. **Preserve policy restrictions.** Middle types receive no coupons by design; common offers vary by period but not household/history; type groups also differ in price sensitivity and habit. These facts affect what the figures establish.
8. **Describe fixed beliefs precisely.** The information-friction exercise does not learn type from purchase outcomes. Keep that fact in main text even if belief vectors and formulas move to the appendix.
9. **Qualify conditional terminal-offer comparisons.** The current figure notes that household composition can contribute to differences. Keep that caveat when using the figure to discuss history dependence.
10. **Use claim-based titles where feasible.** For example, “Personalized offers raise simulated plain revenue by 13.35% relative to no coupons” is more useful than “Results.” State the comparison and avoid attributing the entire gain to one channel.
