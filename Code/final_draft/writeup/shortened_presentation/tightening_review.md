Reviewed the rebuilt 32-slide main sequence, its supporting appendix and current table inputs. The truncated `full_panel_vs_yogurt.tex` is included directly in main slide 13. ## Priority corrections and clarifications

1. **Slide 22, Procedure: controlled versus uncontrolled.** It still says “Use the controlled split fit.” The intended replacement exercise is uncontrolled. Update this sentence and the associated numerical figures together when the new run is recovered; do not simply relabel old controlled results as uncontrolled. State whether the displayed results are the existing three-group run or the new specification.
2. **Slides 4 and 14, HHI: different definitions are now adjacent in the argument.** The opening HHI histogram uses detailed flavor labels. Switching Stats now reports aggregated HHI of 0.78, rather than the detailed-label mean around 0.32. Label or say which grouping each uses. The higher grouped HHI is partly mechanical from combining flavors, not a change in the same concentration statistic. Explicitly introduce berry/plain/other before interpreting the aggregated heatmap on 15.
3. **Slides 14 and 16, opportunities: address weeks above seven packages.** Switching Stats reports 21.72% of weeks above seven packages. The estimator sets outside counts to max(0,7-Q), so total counts are max(7,Q); a literal statement that every estimation week contains exactly seven opportunities is incomplete. Explain the maintained convention once. This also distinguishes the observed estimation counts from the seven-opportunity pricing forecast.
4. **Slide 16, Demand Setting: shared tastes are ambiguous.** “Derive the same base utility from the three flavor categories” can sound as though berry, plain and other have identical intercepts. The model has flavor-specific constants shared across consumers. That is the distinction to make.
5. **Slide 18, Estimation: notation needs repair.** The product over t currently repeats a probability written with y_i rather than a time-indexed observation. Write the per-week count-vector likelihood consistently. The displayed theta description omits phi/habit and is not a complete description of the distribution parameters. These are substantive notation issues rather than merely dense formatting.
6. **Slide 6, Consumer's Problem: normalize at the right point.** The displayed raw outside utility is gamma times an indicator, while the bullet says outside utility is zero. Explain that zero is the normalization after subtracting raw outside utility from both alternatives. Also make q(h) the state sign consistently: the current underbrace spans gamma times the indicator difference, while appendix derivatives use q as the state effect without gamma.
7. **Slides 6 and 17, alpha sign convention.** Theory uses minus alpha times price with alpha positive; empirical demand uses plus alpha_i times price with alpha_i negative. Both are valid, but say that the convention changes so the audience does not infer a sign error.
8. **Slides 20 and 31, WTP: specify the unit.** Approximately $1.75 is the mean WTP effect of a full unit increase in N, holding other predictors fixed; it is not a payment required for every realized mixed-basket switch. The $0.083 stock figure is per unit of cumulative weekly own-flavor share, not automatically per package. This is the novelty interpretation we settled on.
9. **Slides 28–31, information value: answer the second research question.** The conclusion currently reports gains versus no coupons and demand WTP, but does not state the separate value-of-type-information result. Add its validated takeaway after the rerun. Do not present 13.35% as the value of observing type: it is the old personalized-policy versus no-coupon comparison.
10. **Slides 29–30, figure units.** The information-value axis says revenue gain without specifying dollars versus percentage or the consumer/population denominator. Make units explicit when updating the figures. Keep fixed-belief offer optimization distinct from persistent-type revenue evaluation, as slide 28 already does.
