\titleline{Utility and assumptions: switching into plain}
Let $j$ denote plain yogurt and $0$ denote not plain (berry, other yogurt, or no yogurt).
History $h$ is the previous modeled occasion's choice. Set $a=-\alpha>0$.
\[
s_j=0,\quad s_0=1,\qquad
u_j=\beta-ap+s_h\gamma+\varepsilon_j,\qquad u_0=\varepsilon_0.
\]
Repeating plain has no switching term. Entering plain has the signed utility shift $\gamma$:
negative gamma penalizes entry; positive gamma rewards it.
\[
v_j=\beta-ap,\quad v_0=\beta-ap+\gamma,\qquad
WTP_j=\frac{\beta}{a},\quad WTP_0=\frac{\beta+\gamma}{a}.
\]
The subscript on $v_h$ indexes history, not the outside alternative's utility.
\[
q_h(p,\gamma)=\frac{e^{v_h(p,\gamma)}}{1+e^{v_h(p,\gamma)}},\qquad
C_h(p,\gamma)=\frac{\log(1+e^{v_h(p,\gamma)})}{a}.
\]
Shocks are independent Type-I extreme value; their common location constant is omitted.
Consumers choose myopically. Gamma persists across the two pricing periods.
The outside utility level is fixed at zero. Prices are nonnegative, cost is zero and $\delta=1$.

Uniform knows history and the population gamma distribution $G$ but not quintile.
Informed additionally knows quintile and uses its conditional distribution $G_k$.
There is no learning or updating after either choice; the maintained population distribution
is identical across initial histories. Results are conditional on initial history, without household records.

Applied calibration retains the earlier flavor-switching estimates:
$\alpha=-3.530427$, $\beta_{plain}=-1.285109$, original gamma mean $-0.838193$,
SD $2.099165$. The middle 90\% is retained and renormalized; each new quintile has
20\% of retained mass. These estimates are not refitted to the entry-only utility.
The theory calibration is $\alpha=-1$, $\beta=2$, $\gamma\sim N(0,1.25^2)$.

\titleline{Pricing and welfare under entry-only switching}
For either information set, let $F$ be its fixed distribution. Terminal prices solve
\[
p_{2h}\in\arg\max_{p\geq0}\int p q_h(p,\gamma)\,dF(\gamma),\qquad
r_h(\gamma)=p_{2h}q_h(p_{2h},\gamma).
\]
Since $q_j$ does not depend on gamma, $p_{2j}$ is identical across all quintiles and regimes.
For initial history $h_0$, the firm chooses
\[
p_1\in\arg\max_{p\geq0}\int\left[pq_{h_0}(p,\gamma)
+\delta\{q_{h_0}(p,\gamma)r_j(\gamma)
+(1-q_{h_0}(p,\gamma))r_0(\gamma)\}\right]dF(\gamma).
\]
Writing $q_1=q_{h_0}(p_1,\gamma)$, an interior optimum satisfies
\[
\int\left[q_1+\alpha q_1(1-q_1)
\{p_1+\delta[r_j(\gamma)-r_0(\gamma)]\}\right]dF(\gamma)=0.
\]
At $p_1=0$, the derivative must be nonpositive. Initial-inside prices can still differ
across quintiles because future outside-state revenues differ, even though current inside demand does not.
\begin{align*}
CS(\gamma,h_0)&=C_{h_0}(p_1,\gamma)+\delta\{q_1 C_j(p_{2j},\gamma)+(1-q_1)C_0(p_{20},\gamma)\},\\
R(\gamma,h_0)&=p_1q_1+\delta\{q_1r_j(\gamma)+(1-q_1)r_0(\gamma)\},\\
TW(\gamma,h_0)&=CS(\gamma,h_0)+R(\gamma,h_0).
\end{align*}
Quintile outcomes integrate these expressions over $G_k$ using the appropriate regime's policy.
All welfare and revenue figures report $100(X^{I}-X^{U})/|X^{U}|$; a zero baseline is undefined.

This specification changes demand relative to the earlier $s_j=-1$, $s_0=1$ model.
Prices, probabilities, revenue and welfare are therefore recomputed, rather than only shifting a welfare baseline.
