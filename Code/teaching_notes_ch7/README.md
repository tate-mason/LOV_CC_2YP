## Teaching approach

Assumed preparation: students can read supply and demand graphs, solve a simple linear equation, and interpret marginal benefit and marginal cost. Class 1 includes a short refresher. No calculus is required.

“Ease of translation” is interpreted as short, direct English that is easy to paraphrase or translate orally. Use one idea per sentence. Keep technical terms consistent. Say the full term before using its abbreviation. Read equations in words. Each lesson provides suggested spoken explanations, drawing steps, student prompts, answers, and a timed exit check. Times include student thinking and board writing; optional material is outside the 55-minute plan.

Use the slides as anchors. During calculations, leave the relevant slide up and work on the board. Do not read every slide bullet. The examples mostly preserve the numbers in the Chapter 7 notes so students can review the same calculations afterward. New practice examples and tables are teaching illustrations, not empirical estimates.

## Accuracy notes for the slide deck

- **Slides 11–12:** internalizing a cost requires a change in incentives. Awareness alone may not change behavior. A tax matches social marginal cost when it matches marginal external damage; not every rule automatically creates that equality.
- **Slide 15:** the external benefit exists before a subsidy. A subsidy changes private incentives. Distinguish the social benefit curve from private willingness to pay.
- **Slide 19:** a fence is efficient only when its cost is below the damage it prevents, assuming no cheaper remedy. Clearly assigned rights do not imply that someone must build a fence.
- **Slide 21:** rivalry means one person's use reduces availability or quality for others. Several people can use a congested road at once and still impose rival costs.
- **Slides 26–28:** specify access rules and congestion. A gym is approximately a club good when uncrowded; scarce machines can be rival.
- **Slides 30–33:** distinguish open access from a shared resource managed by enforceable community rules. Shared ownership does not make collapse inevitable.
- **Slide 35:** trading can minimize the cost of meeting a cap. Whether the cap is socially efficient is a separate question.
- **Slides 3 and 36:** some remaining pollution can be efficient in an interior solution. Full elimination can also be efficient when the benefits of the final cleanup unit exceed its cost.
